# The Science of Critical Thinking

The article's second half argues that **science shows ways to keep the brain sharp** in the age of AI — i.e., critical thinking is a trainable, measurable skill, not a fixed trait.

## What the cited literature supports

- **Cognitive training works:** the 2002 *Cognitive Instruction* study (Remy, Schmidt & Boshuizen) on instructional design for cognitive skills is the oldest citation — evidence that structured practice improves thinking.
- **AI changes the task landscape:** Bastani et al. (*PNAS* 2025) and Yan et al. (*Nature Reviews Psychology* 2025) situate AI's impact on how people learn and think.
- **Measuring the effect:** Chesire et al. (*Trials* 2024) — an RCT-style study on AI-assisted learning; Kops et al. (*Computers in Human Behavior* 2025); Mendoza et al. (*Media Asia* 2024); Axelsson et al. (*Journal of Research in Technology in Education* 2023) — AI tools in education and their effects.
- **The mechanism:** McGrew (*Current Opinion in Psychology* 2024) and Eliseev & Marsh (*Applied Cognitive Psychology* 2023) address how offloading to AI (e.g., memory tasks) changes learning.

## Practical implication

- Keep **deliberate practice** in the loop: retrieval practice, source evaluation, and argument construction are the "workouts" for memory and reasoning.
- Use AI as a **sparring partner** (generating counterarguments, asking "why") rather than as a **substitute** for doing the thinking yourself.
